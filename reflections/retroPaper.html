from paper to play

Working on this group activity helped me better understand paper prototypes and the complexity involved in constructing them. What initially appeared simple required far more careful consideration than I anticipated.

This process, which I had no prior experience with, was an invaluable learning opportunity. I began approaching the task with algorithmic thinking, visualising how a computer might process the various components—such as the game’s background setup, moving characters, and interactive props. Each element needed to be flexible enough to respond dynamically when the user interacted with the
paper prototype.

This experience not only deepened my understanding of
paper prototyping but also equipped me with the skills necessary to create my own, with a focus on designing for both functionality and user engagement.

The exercise has taught me to appreciate the intricate planning required to bring an interactive system to life, where every component must be carefully designed to simulate the real experience.
